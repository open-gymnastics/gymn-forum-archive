LeeAnn Writes and many others respond regarding this subject, but the whole
story has not yet emerged.:
>I read on Prodigy that Chainey Umphrey, Drew Durbin, Chris Waller, and Jair
>Lynch have petitioned onto the World Team Trial in October.


The men listed above were all on  the National team for 1993.   Because
they were all on the National team they are allowed to petition to be on
the National team for 1994, until the Winter Nationals in January.  At that
time if their injuries do not allow them to compete they will be off the
National team.  Rachele has already listed the World Championship team -
Just a note that the women could learn from the men and make the National's
the place where the team is picked rather than the trials being held in
October.  If you remember correctly it is the trials that Steve Nunno
doesn't want Shannon to compete in.  Sounds like he would like her to be on
the World Team if they could have a couple of months to train for that
instead of the trials.

anyway, the men will compete again for the 8th through 14th spots in
January, inother words, those who made the World's team do not need to
compete again for a spot on the National Team until next years summer
Nationals.  Until the January competition, the men will be asked to be in
meets in the ranking order.  In other words USA Gymnaestrada in October
will first be open to the World's team, if they don't get a 7 man team
there then they invite the men who competed at Nationals and landed spots 8
- 14.  If the team is still incomplete then they will go to the four who
petitioned on.  From there  - well, they have no where else to go.

Someone doesn't know Drew Durbin - he is fabulous on Pommel Horse, for the
US (I add this only to deter any fights regarding this post), was at ST.
Petersburg with Scott, Chainey and Blaine.  He and Blaine are from Ohio
State University.  Drew has an  East Asian heritage, and is still on the
slight side (very little bulking in the muscle department).

I hope the information on Prodigy is now recognized for what it is -
misinformed.

Being in Nashville and being a men's gym fan I can tell you that little was
worth showing about the men (though I think that they should have at least
showed results instead of pretending they don't exist at all).  There was
only an hour and a lot of great women's stuff was also missed due to simple
lack of time. The Jr. women were pretty great too and they never see air
either (unless of course they sit on Nadia's lap ;-).

The only men's sets in the meet worth mentioning were Mihai's pommels (EF),
Roth's HB (*huge* Gaylord 2 and the highest score of the men's meet with a
9.9) in AA, Keswick's rings in AA (EF was decent too), and McCain's butt (I
know for a fact they have camera shots of it!) ... Steve's routine's (EF
p-bars for one) are nice too though he fell on HB (his best event) in both AA
and EF.  Of course, Steve and Mihai are notable for being the only American
guys who can point their toes and hit a handstand ... they look pretty good
even when they fall. *Everyone* had at least one major error in either compos
or optionals and the level was overall rather dismal.

Not only were the guys mentally off but physically they were struggling as
well. Roethlisberger hurt his ankle midway through AA. Chainey suffered from
an ear infection that totally threw off his balance (4 falls in optional PH
and then he scratched). Jair broke his hand last week. Durbin is still
suffering shoulder trauma. Chris Waller hurt his back earlier this month (a
different injury then the one that kept him from the qualifying comp.).
Another Waller note: Chris is also scheduled (though I'm not sure that was
the best way to put it) to become a Daddy for the first time in January (wife
Cindy also has some part of the process) and said that he didn't really mind
missing out on the world team (unlike women men have no seperate trials and
no peitioning) "just in case she goes into labor early or anything ... this
is a bigger deal in my life right now." Billy Roth suffered food poisoning
and was forced to pull out of most of the event finals. The one he did
attempt  - vault - he landed on a camera man (the slo-mo of that was pretty
great).

Back to Mihai for a sec ... he (and all the Gold Cup gymnasts) wore little
round bandaids with magnets that supposedly have some sort of metaphysical
energy force thing. Well it *was* Mihai's best meet ever (that USOF debacle
is behind him now) and as Ed Burch said "I think it's all up here [in their
heads] but hey if it works why not?"

Date: Mon, 29 Aug 1994 07:49:19 -0400 (EDT)
From: ***@delphi.com
Subject: USA Champs; Women's AA

Women's Senior Nationals
Optional Session
Friday, Aug 26 1994
Municipal Auditorium
Nashville, TN
Attendance: 4700

The women's session began with the introduction of the new
mascot -- it looks wierd, like an orange sock that exploded in the
dryer.  You saw it on TV, no doubt.  Anyways, the crowd seemed
to have a pretty good response to it (too good, actually -- you'd
never guess there were only 4700 people in that arena with the
noise that those girls were generating, to get the free t-shirts the
mascot was giving away).  The mascot hasn't been named yet --
that was a contest for people attending the meet.

ROTATION ONE

There was a lot of anticipation before the girls hit the floor...
would Shannon Miller defend her title, or would Dominique
Dawes finally beat her?  Well, everyone had come to Tennessee to
find out, so let's begin.

Shannon Miller (Dynamo) hit her bars routine, although her
releases were tentative and her double layout was loose and
"whippy".  Her "cleap hip half  Healy to straddle back" (words of a
coach next to me -- it's her 1.5 pirouette with straddled release to
the low bar, in slightly  plainer English) was hit well, however,
and looked really cool.

Rachel Rochelli (Cypress) hit her Shushunova (full-twisting
Tkatchev) on bars, but then missed a sky-high (but unrotated)
Tkatchev... what a pity.  A Shushunova would have been a treat in
event finals (especially because I missed seeing it here and only
know about it because other people talked about it!).
 
Marianna Webster (Dynamo) joined the "nifty bars" club with a
front giant, full, front giant (otherwise known as front giant,
Healy -- or is that Healy, front giant?  I think the first is correct)
sequence.

Wendy Bruce (Brown's) showed the exact same floor routine from
'89 -- or at least mostly the same.  The choreography was
incredibly similar to that last one.  Her tumbling included a full-in
mount; a 2.5 twister in the second pass, and front tumbling
combinations for her last pass.  It wasn't totally smooth -- her feet
stuttered here and there, but it was a really fun routine. 

Amy Chow (West Valley) made you look twice by grinning
throughout her classical music routine (as if there isn't enough
reason to look twice at her already).  But still, a smile is great, and
her tumbling was strong with a piked full-in mount.  She's a bit
loose though in her execution.

Dawes (Hill's) landed her vaults -- the 1.5 twisting Yurchenko's. 
Awesome.  Crossed feet and a little bit bent in the legs, but still
way cool.

Jennie Thompson (Dynamo) hit a good floor with nice split leaps,
triple twists for both mount and dismount (the first was out of
three whips to a ff); and a piked full-in for her middle pass.  The
world's tiniest full-in, no doubt.

Kellee Davis (American Twisters) displayed a great double layout
on floor.  So did Lisa Gianni (North Stars) and Kristi Lichey
(Cincinnatti). (Not in this rotation...) None of those three made
floor finals.  Sarah Cain (GI Twisters) and Amanda Borden
(Cinc) did Arabian double fronts and both made finals.  So, what
skill should we all learn?

Borden, generally speaking, was much improved.  Watching her
was a lot of fun as she displayed poise and confidence on every
event.  Particularly on beam, her moves really flowed well from
one to the next.

Eileen Diaz (Cypress) hit her great laid out full-in dismount off
bars.

Kristi Lichey displayed an awesome ff to three layouts on beam.

ROTATION TWO

Rotation one had gone well, with most of the girls knocking
through their routines and giving a great showof gymnastics.  The
mood for this rotation was set almost immediatly when the
absolutely unthinkable happened: Miller, who is normally so
consistent that it becomes boring, put her hands down on her full-
in off beam.  She also looked a bit sluggish throughout the
routine, but she did hit all of her skills.  When Miller put her
hands down, the whole arena was amazed, and the atmosphere
changed from then on.  The crowd, who seemed partial to Dawes
even from the start, really began pulling for Dominique on each
event.  From most people's perspective, the competition was, at
that moment, effectively over.

Soon after Miller's routine, Dawes mounted bars and rocked her
release-less routine (Well, she has the Shaposhnikova, but really). 
The crowd went WILD.

Borden was on floor soon after (these three girls always performed
within minutes of each other due to consistent rotation orders),
and hit the best Arabian double front I've seen her do -- landed it
really well.  (Nice to see her not take that one step forward.)

Meanwhile, another outstanding Amanda, Ms. Curry (Cypress)
hit a cool Rulfova (full-twisting back handspring swingdown)
polishing off a really well executed beam.

Sarah Cain, who has really nice presentation on all events, hit a
great second vault of Yurchenko 1/2 turn to pike front.  I really
did think it was supposed to be a pike front, too.  But a nearby
coach told me that no, the number she called was a layout.  The
judges apparently saw a layout too, and so didn't deduct.  Hmmm. 
Nevertheless, regardless of rules and all, it really was an
outstanding vault.

ROTATION THREE

Amy Chow had lots of cool front swinging work on bars, and -
boom- landed her full-in full-out.  Outstanding!

Jennie T also had nice front swinging, a wrong-way Tkatchev to a
somewhat strange transition (you had to see it -- sort of a delayed
drop glide), and a double layout dismount that was better than
Shannon's (but then again, all the double layout dismounts off of
bars were better than Shannon's).

Dawes, coming around the turn on her victory lap, hit her three
layouts on beam and her full-in dismount with one small step. 
The crowd was about to explode by now.  I've never felt so much
crowd support for a gymnast.  Well, sure, Zmeskal couldn't walk
into an arena without screaming girls; however, this crowd really
was pulling for Dominique in a way that I've just never seen
before.

Borden hit a high piked front with her legs straight and locked
tight together.  Steve Elliot, an asst coach at Cincinatti now, no
doubt had something to do with her improvement in this event.

The best triple twists on floor belonged to Amanda Curry.  No
"two and seven eighths" with this girl.  Everyone on press row
really seemed to notice her.

Summer Reid has amazing presence on both beam and floor.  I'd
heard before that she had a really admired style, but saw this
myself in person and was duly impressed.  She especially rocked
beam in finals (see that report later) and was totally robbed of a
medal (in my opinion).

Martha Grubbs (Parkettes) threw a double twisting Yurchenko. 
And it was pretty good, too.  Zmeskal, Okino, and Campi, all
seemed to be awed by it. 

Rachel Rochelli had the uneviable task of following Shannon
Miller on each event.  Miller hit her 1992 routine in the same way
she always does (consistency or boring?  You decide), and the
crowd showed its appreciation with lots of noise.  (While they
were excited to see Dawes win for once, they were all still Miller
fans, for sure.)

ROTATION FOUR

In this rotation, we saw every Cincinatti girl throw the best
routine of her life on bars.  Or at least it seemed that way.  They
all have cool bars and great line on this event.  Borden flew
through her routine, double front dismount, STICK.  Jaycie
Phelps, boom, good dismount.  Kristi Lichey gets up -- swing
swing swing -- double layout (tight) -blam -- stick.  It was really a
lot of fun.

Dawes was one of the first girls to go on floor (maybe even the
first one).  To continue my analogies, she broke into a full run on
the back stretch of her victory lap, and didn't slow down a bit
when she saw that finish line.  I saw Dawes do one of her best
floors ever and have an absolute ball while she was doing so.  The
judges loved it too -- 9.925.

Miller was then on vault.  With her score, Dominique had put the
title out of reach of Shannon.  I doubt that Ms. Miller knew that at
this time, but I'm sure she knew that tonight was just not going to
happen for her (in terms of a gold medal -- her routines really
were fine).  So, all that's left is showing the crowd and the TV
audience (and the judges and other gymnasts and coaches) that
she still had it in her.  She got it out of her with two stuck vaults -
- which made her happy as she's never stuck those vaults in
competition.  After the first one, Steve, who was elated with her
first vault, yelled to no one in particular (but certainly loud
enough for the judges to hear) "Hey, what's the harm in giving a
ten when you see one, eh?"  He said it jokingly, though -- not like
he was berating the judges or anything.  For her second vault,
Miller hit again, and was scored a bit higher (9.90 on the first,
9.95 on the second).  I thought her scores were almost completely
because of her name, however -- her vaults were certainly good,
and the landings were incredible (these are forward landings
remember), but the vaults just weren't good enough to be marked
that high.  As I've said before though -- I'm not a judge, what do I
know?  By this point she was in second place no matter what, so
it's more of an issue about the integrity of gymnastics scoring
rather than a pressing matter relative to this particular
competition -- and that's a thread for another day.

Beam was a slow event in this rotation, and so everyone had the
opportunity to watch both Amy Chow and Jennie T.  Chow
displayed a ff, layout, ff, layout; a standing full (break); a Rulfova;
and a dismount of triple twist.  Someone needs to tell her that it's
hard to twist on beam, as she seems to not understand that.  =) 
Jennie then also displayed a standing full that was DRILLED into
the beam (she used to do a Rulfova too -- wonder what happened
to that move).  She was really solid throughout her routine -- but
then again, she's been known to be a beam phenom since 1992
when she defeated Zmeskal, Strug, and I think Okino at the
Alamo Invite in San Antonio, TX -- weeks before Zmeskal won
her world beam title.  She dismounted with a full-in that was
easily completed, if I remember correctly.  I just hope she's not
peaking too early!

And thus ended the night, the story being the major and yet
somehow expected victory of Dawes.  People on press row were
even talking on Thursday night (the women's AA was Friday
night) that if Dawes won, they would write this and that for their
stories, etc.  Other notes:

Dynamo had the most gymnasts qualified to Nationals with eight. 
However, two (Heather Brink and Tanya Maiers) scratched. 
Tanya was diagnosed with pleurisy.  No word on what happened
with Heather.  The next power gyms were Cypress, Parkettes, and
Cincinatti, all with six gymnasts each.

The most gymnasts (men and women together) were from Texas -
- 15!  Florida is a distant second with nine gymnasts.

Larissa Fontaine had elbow surgery just six weeks before the meet. 
She scratched after compulsories.

Sammie Muhleman pulled the skin off her knee on beam (I didn't
see it happen) in the first rotation on Friday night.  It required 45
stitches!

Rachele
