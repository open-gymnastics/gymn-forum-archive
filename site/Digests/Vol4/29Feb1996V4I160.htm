Date:    Thu, 29 Feb 1996 08:46:23 -0400
From:    ***@CAPITALNET.COM
Subject: Re: Canadian levels

Debbie wrote:
>Can anyone from Canada tell me what a Level 9 in Canadian women's gymn is?
> What would this be comparable to in the US?  What skills are on this level?

Don't know where to find a Level 9 around here but...

In artistic women's gymn in Canada, the National Stream categories are
Novice, Open, Junior High Performance, and Senior High Performance.
Each province has their own Provincial Stream.  I don't know how all the
provinces handle their provincial stream, but here are my impressions on
Ontario vs. Massachusetts (basically the only 2 places where I've lived) in
skill level & performance:

Optionals but not compulsories are competed in Ontario. Hopefully, this may
help U.S. clubs who may be interested in hosting Ontario clubs for
invitationals and vice versa.

Level 1 (Pre-Novice 9 & 10 yrs old 1st year, Novice 10 & 11 yrs old, Junior
12 & 13, Senior 14 & up):
 - may only perform FIG A & B skills, C, D, & E skills are restricted, no bonus
 - only 1A & 1B (other than Pre-Novice) vaults may be performed
 - don't need more than 8 elements, 1 bar change, A dismount on bars
 - acro series doesn't need flight, A dismount on beam
 - 2 salto line allowed but not required, gym series of min 3 A's on floor
 - generally well-executed and well-composed routines
 - equivalent to Level 5-7 in performance in Mass.

Level 2 (Pre-Novice, Novice, Junior, Senior, Open):
 - no round-off entry to vault, beam, bars, no bonus
 - D & E skills are restricted, C skills restricted for Pre-Novices
 - only Senior & Open have FIG special requirements
 - vaults are typically handspring-full, half-full, sometimes half-1.5
 - bars usually include usual B flights, giants (Junior & up)
 - typically don't see flight acro series on beam, usually front salto dismounts
 - about 50% of the competitors perform layout full, front to back salto
tumbling on floor, double saltos are restricted
 - generally very well-executed and well-composed routines
 - equivalent to Level 7-9 in performance in Mass.

Level 3 (Pre-Novice, Novice, Junior, Senior):
 - only Senior 3 competes full FIG no restrictions
 - Yurchenko vaults, D & E skills are restricted (except Sr 3)
 - C's not required until Junior, double saltos restricted until Junior
 - handspring-1.5, half-1.5 vaults, some Tsuk tuck & pike, occasional front tuck
 - giants with pirouette, occasional front giant, nobody D releases
 - double turns on beam, some ff-ff connections, simple mixed series, saltos
 - a few double saltos on floor from Juniors & Seniors, double fulls
 - medium-valued skills, well-executed and well-composed routines
 - athletes who try out for National Stream in November and don't make 66.0
for 2 days of FIG Ib, end up in Level 3
 - equivalent to Level 9-10 in skill level and performance in Mass.

There is also Ontario Pre-Novice 4 level which is like the US Elite
Childrens' level (if that's still around).  They compete 4A & 4B, no bonus,
FIG special requirements, and only Yurchenko vaults and E's are restricted.

In many regions in Ontario, there are recreational competitive levels as well.
I have noticed that many of the routines in rec-comp have similar content.
So compared to US compulsories in skill level & performance, this is where
you would see equivalence to US Levels 4-5. Girls that do US Level 1-3
skills don't normally compete around here.

Hope this helps.  And I hope you weren't referring to RSG  ;^)...

Regards,

Grace

the U.S. Championships.  There is one more meet in which a gymnast can
