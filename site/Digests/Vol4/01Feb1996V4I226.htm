Subject: The Latest Discussion Topics

        I have been busy lately, but I wanted to comment on some of the
things being discussed lately on the list.
        First, the discussion of Olympic Team coach for the U.S. Women.
If Bela has Dominique and Kerri on the team he should be the coach.  I think
the Bela/Steve fight is over so he would probably would be fair.  Bela is
simply the most experienced.
        Second, this Kim Zmeskal question.  I am a big fan but I think it
is very premature to put her on a prediction list to make the U.S. team not
to mention individual medals.  Remember she has not competed an amateur meet
in a long time.  I think its great she is trying, but I have not seen
anything to convince me she has a chance.
        Lastly, gymnastics vs. figure skating question.  Figure skating
had the Tonya/Nancy mess.  This proved very profitable.  More importantly
USAG does a terriable job promoting meets.  They NEVER try to reach out
beyond the gymnastics community to increase attendance.  Also meets need to
be different.  Make them faster, have less particpants, sign famous gymnasts
to contracts so they have to come (Shannon Miller at the Goodwill Games),
and stick with and easy format that anyone can understand.

        Just my .02 cents,

        Mikester Bee

Date:    Wed, 31 Jan 1996 21:32:39 -0500
From:    ***@GNATNET.NET
Subject: Write CBS!

I am posting this message to urge EVERY SINGLE MEMBER of Gymn to take 5
minutes to e-mail CBS regarding this weeks The Client episode.  Even
those of us who missed the episode have read enough about the episode
here on Gymn to understand the ridiculous stereotypes that The Client
created and perpetuated in the episode.  Ten or twenty messages will not
make a difference to CBS executives, but 650 might.  We want CBS to know
that we will not stand for their spreading their ignorance of our sport
to the American public.  We want them and the public to know that
gymnasts whose parents or coaches want their success more than they do
themselves simply do not make it to high levels of competition.  Nothing
can compel a person to dedicate themselves mentally and physically to
the goal of excellence except for true love for gymnastics.  Other
stereotypes may exist in perhaps 1% of elite gymnasts, but certainly not
every stereotype in a single athlete.  In addition, these problems are
NOT unique to gymnastics, but are found in the top levels of all sports,
and are being addressed and fought throughout the gymnastics community.

Before you compose your message, sit a moment and reflect on the reasons
that you love gymnastics.  Think about the valuable lessons that the
sport teaches young people.  Think about the sense of accomplishment a
5-year-old gains when he or she completes his or her first cartwheel.

I hope that I have gotten each one of you a little fired up and made you
realize that the only way to get this message across is together.  My
letter to CBS will not make a difference, but all of our letters can.

The address at which you can contact CBS is: marketing@cbs.com

Happy gymming,
Kerry
