From:    ***@MAGNUS.ACS.OHIO-STATE.EDU
Subject: "bird" music

     Yes, Oksana Omelianchik did use chirpy "bird" music - to GREAT success -
in 1985-6. But it was not the same piece of music that Sazonenkova, Sheremeta
and others have used. Which I think is very wise. Oksana's routine was
one-of-a-kind, and if anyone tried to use the same music, I think they'd just
be inviting negative comparisons. NO ONE could ever interpret that piece as
well as Omelianchik did.
     I feel the same way about Natalia Frolova's 1986 floor routine. Her
choreography fit the music SO perfectly, and no one who's come after her and
used that music has come even close to the magic of her routine. When I watched
Natalia Kalinina try to use that music in 1989, it nearly made me cringe. The
coaches seem to have agreed, since she had a new routine within 6 months.
     About what Kathy Johnson Clarke said about floor choreography, I agreed
with her 100%. But I can see how television viewers could have a problem with
what she said. I think the problem is that a lot of the really
well-choreographed routines she was talking about and comparing to Gogean's and
Milo's did not get shown. But Kathy had seen them all week in qualifying and
semi-finals. And she *did* say she was referring to the judges' previous
decisions, i.e., the decisions they'd made in qualifying and semi-finals. In my
opinion, the French girls had the best choreography by far. Furnon was amazing!
Roza Galiyeva also has a really cool routine, and I thought Vasiliki
Tsavdaridou (sp?) of Greece was also very good. So was Joanna Juarez of Spain.
But ABC didn't show any of them, which made Kathy's comments sound a little
odd. I personally was quite entertained by Gemma Paz. Sure, it was a lot of
bouncing around, but some notable American gymnasts (Zmeskal, Moceanu) did/do a
lot of bouncing around too. But whereas I often found their routines too
"cutsie," Paz's didn't seem like a circus act. I'm not saying it was the
greatest thing I've ever seen, but I thought it showed more personality than
Gogean's and Milosovici. I basically like Milo, but she seemed bored to tears.
And Gina doesn't exactly display an abundance of personality. I didn't think
Sheremeta did either. With music like that, I believe you should smile, simply
as part of the presentation. Having been in PR, I can say that one reason the
Chinese girl got such a response was that she SMILED and really got the crowd
behind her. So did Furnon, Paz and Tsavdaridou. There were some other gymnasts,
notably Kochetkova and Mirgorodskaya, who had beautifully choreographed
routines (especially Anna), but they didn't seem to put their souls into what
they were doing, and it ended up leaving the crowd "dead." If I had had my way,
Ludivine Furnon would be floor champion. But she had to perform first and
wasn't stable on all her landings. She has it all though - personality,
choreography and tumbling. Few can say that.
     Just my $.02.

Beth

Date:    Tue, 30 Apr 1996 23:50:04 EDT
From:    ***@COMPUSERVE.COM
Subject: 96 Worlds: Top Ten Highlights

Hello to all,

Here is my second installment of top ten lists for the 96 Worlds held in San
Juan, Puerto Rico. Today s installment is my list of top highlights from Worlds.
Again, these are in no particular order. Actually, as I have read other posts
concerning Worlds, there are many more items I could add to this list of
highlights, but for the sake of brevity I will limit it to ten.

1. Dominique Dawes (USA) earns a bronze on the beam. After a very disappointing
event final on uneven bars, Dominique came back the next day like a pro and did
a fantastic balance beam routine. I can't believe she can still do three back
flips in a row considering how much taller she is now than a few years ago.

2. Yuri Chechi (ITA) earns his fourth gold on rings. What can one say? His look
to the judges when he completed his routine said it all. It was if he was
saying: "There, I dare you to find a deduction."

3. Igor Korobchinsky (UKR) makes a comeback after his leg injury in Sabae. I was
surprised to see him compete at all. Admittedly his routines were still shaky,
although he did make semi-finals on p-bars. But, it was great to see he has
recovered and is making a comeback.

4. Chris LaMorte (USA) made finals on rings. Of the US athletes at Worlds, I
found Chris to be the most affable. He was willing to talk about his routine and
about the judging. He also kept a real good attitude throughout the competition.
So, personally, I was glad to see he made the finals. US fans in attendance
obviously felt he was underscored (9.637), but he did have trouble coming out of
an L-cross and hopped on his landing.

5.  Ivan Ivankov (BLR) did not medal in any of his event finals, but he did put
in four solid routines: floor 9.550, pommels 9.650, rings 9.675, and p-bars
9.725. Not shabby at all. Such consistency is the sign of someone who has a shot
at an all-around medal in Atlanta. Provided, of course, he gets his act together
on high bar.

6. Kui Yuanyuan (CHN) earns a gold on floor. I admit I am no expert on judging
women's floor, but it did seem like she was someone who excelled at combining
tumbling with choreography. While other routines looked like someone jumping
around to music, hers looked like a performance.

7. Chainey Umphrey (USA) does a solid hi-bar routine in finals. Chainey didn't
medal (9.712), but he did a great job in my opinion. His only noticeable flaw
was the hop on the landing. Also, I don't know if they mentioned this on
television, but the day after hi-bar finals, Chainey took his exams to get into
medical school this coming fall. Can you imagine: preparing for Worlds and a
medical school entrance exam at the same time?

8. Krasimir Dounev (BUL) earns a silver on hi-bar. Krasi did five releases and
totally stuck his landing. I didn't really know this guy prior to Worlds, but he
was one of the nicest athletes at Worlds, so one couldn't help but root for him
to do well. Also, the name Krasimir apparently means "beautiful world." I think
most women who have seen this guy would agree that this is an appropriate name
for the 23-year-old Bulgarian.

9.  Aljaz Pegan (SLO) also did a great hi-bar routine. In fact, he had the
leading score after semi-finals (9.725) and actually did even better in finals
(9.750) but just missed a medal.  His gaylord with a twist was a highlight of
the hi-bar competition.

10. Vasiliki Tsavdaridou (GRE) on vault did not earn a medal (9.518), but was
still great to watch.  She had a smile on her face as soon as she landed, which
made you think, that some gymnasts do enjoy the sport they are in. She was also
the gymnast who had to wait about 15 minutes during the qualifying competition
while the judges made up their minds about some inconsequential vault score. So,
I was glad she didn't let the pressure of waiting get to her.

Whew, that's enough for that list. One other note, though. I think it was better
attending Worlds than going to the Olympics this summer. The views of the
competition areas was much better than it will be in Atlanta. And, it was much
easier meeting the athletes than it will be in Atlanta. Also, it wasn't as hot
and humid in San Juan as it will be in Atlanta!

Take care, and I'll be reporting next on honorable mentions for the Worlds.
Dana

   Okay, here's my introduction. I'm John C, and I've been on
Gymn for almost three years. My background in gymnastics is nil;
my rural Illinois heritage prevented anything but seeing the
Olympics on T.V. every four years. :)  The first live competition
I got to see was when USGF Nationals were here in Minneapolis
in 1989. Since then I've managed to get to several other Nationals
and to Worlds in Indianapolis. And to Worlds this month, with
the I.G. tour! Highly recommended. I'm constantly amazed at the
detailed descriptions of routines that I read on this list, as well
as references to performances from many years ago. My eye isn't
that sharp (yet).

after the previous fellow had to stop. Last year Rachele created an
outstanding program for maintaining the calendar, and it generates
the nifty Web page versions too. Anyway, the May calendar will be
on its way to you in a few minutes.
